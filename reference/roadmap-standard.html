Campaign Ops · 13 August 2026

Five roadmaps in five months

Every version that has existed, what each one locked, and what survived. Then the format worth standardising on — which is mostly the one we already have.
There is no early-July roadmap. Not in the Ops folder, not in the repo. What was live in July was the 21 May Phase 1 checklist reading straight from Airtable, and nothing was ever frozen — so there is no July state to go back to. That is the actual problem, and it is a snapshot problem more than a format problem.

The good news, once you line them up: the underlying facts have barely changed. Every version has been carrying the same four things under different clothes. The churn has been in presentation, not in structure.

The proposal

Lock the row, not the page

Every version above was carrying the same four facts. The rebuilds happened because each new page invented its own way of storing them, so nothing could be carried forward. Fix that once and the presentation can change as often as it needs to.

The standard row — every roadmap view renders from this
lane
One of the seven. Adding a lane is a decision, not a side effect — if work does not fit, that is worth noticing rather than routing around.
item
One line, plain language, says what it does. No object-nouns — the same rule that fixed the seventeen idea titles.
status
To Do · In Progress · Done · Blocked. Four states, no more. Blocked is the one that earns its place — it is the only way a roadmap tells you something you did not already know.
owner
A named person, never a team. Blank is a valid answer and should look like one, because an unowned item is a real finding.
order
Position within the lane. Boring, and the reason two views of the same data never disagree.
demo optional
Link to the thing the item produced. This is what makes it a record of work rather than a list of intentions.
Airtable stays the source. The page is a rendering, never the record. Any view that cannot be regenerated from the base is a one-off, and one-offs get archived after the meeting rather than becoming the seventh format.
Stage-Gate stops being called a roadmap. It is the operating model and it should live as its own reference page. Filing it as a roadmap is traceable to four rebuilds in one week.
Freeze a copy before every Fleck check-in. reference/snapshots/roadmap-YYYY-MM-DD.html Thirty seconds, and next time the question is “what did July look like” there is an answer.
Two things worth arguing with

Before this gets locked

Seven lanes may already be six. Lane 07 is A&R Admin and A&R Ops Gaps bolted together, and lane 03 carries three things in its name. That is usually a sign the lane was drawn around who was in the room rather than around the work. Worth a look before freezing them — freezing the wrong seven is worse than not freezing.
“Blocked” is a political word. Four-state status means the roadmap will visibly say a department is blocking something. That is the whole value, and it is also exactly the turf-anxiety trigger. Suggest the blocked state names what is missing rather than who owes it — “waiting on production access” rather than “waiting on IT”.

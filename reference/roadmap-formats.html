Five roadmap formats, all showing your actual Phase 1

Same 45 items, same seven lanes, five shapes. These are the formats real product teams use, rendered with your rows rather than described in the abstract — so you can point at one instead of us guessing at each other. Every panel says what it buys you and what it costs you.

Now / Next / Later

ProdPad · ProductPlan · Roadmunk
What it is

The default modern product roadmap. Three buckets, no dates. Invented specifically because executives kept reading dated roadmaps as commitments and then holding teams to them.

What it buys you

Answers the one question a check-in actually asks: what are you doing right now, and what is after that. Turf-safe — it describes work, not people.

What it costs you

The buckets are a judgement call every fortnight, and you have to defend them. It also hides the long tail: 30 items in Later looks like 30 items nobody is doing.

Outcome-first

Melissa Perri · Teresa Torres · Basecamp
What it is

Lead with the change you are buying, then list the steps underneath as evidence. The headline is a sentence, not a noun.

What it buys you

This is the only format where a reader who knows nothing can tell why the work matters. Also the strongest turf defence you have: an outcome belongs to the label, not to a department.

What it costs you

It takes real writing, and it is the slowest to update. Wrong outcomes are worse than no outcomes — you will be arguing about the sentence, not the work.

Board / Kanban

Linear · Jira · Trello · Monday
What it is

Columns are status. Every item is a card that moves left to right over time.

What it buys you

Movement is the whole point — between two check-ins you can literally see cards move. Universally understood; nobody needs it explained.

What it costs you

Look at yours. 42 cards stacked in To Do and two other columns empty. A board is honest about a pipeline that is not flowing, which may or may not be what you want in the room.

Swimlane × time

Aha! · Roadmunk · Jira Plans · the classic Gantt
What it is

Rows are your lanes, columns are time. The most requested format by executives and the one most product teams have spent fifteen years trying to get away from.

What it buys you

It is the only shape that answers "when". If Fleck asks when something lands, this is the page that has an answer on it.

What it costs you

Every cell is a promise. Put a thing in September and you have committed to September in front of your boss. Empty rows read as dead lanes even when they are just unscheduled.

Grouped list

Linear list view · GitHub Projects · what you have now
What it is

Fixed lanes, one line per item, exceptions marked. The format currently live at reference/roadmap.html.

What it buys you

Highest information density per inch, survives being printed, and the lane spine has been stable since 21 May — that stability is the most valuable thing you own here.

What it costs you

It is a register, not a roadmap. It records what exists; it does not say what is next or why. That is likely the mismatch you are feeling.

One honest caveat. Three of these formats need a field your data does not have yet. Now / Next / Later needs a bucket per item, and the swimlane needs a month — in these demos both are derived from the existing order number purely so the shape is visible. They are illustrative, not a claim about sequence. Choosing either format means deciding those values on purpose. The board and the grouped list run on the data exactly as it stands today.

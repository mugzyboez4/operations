The guide behind the tracker

Where the numbers come from

Every deadline on the tracker is one of two things: a confirmed floor from our own delivery process, or a planning target built on standard industry lead times.

← Back to the tracker

01

Two kinds of numbers

Confirmed floors Confirmed

The absolute-latest dates, set internally with Release Planning (Jive delivery process, July 2026). Miss these and the release physically can't go right. Marked green on the tracker.

Planning targets

Deliberately conservative dates built from standard industry lead times — pressing-plant queues, distribution processing, clearance turnarounds. Earlier is always safe.

02

The confirmed floors

Three dates are hard. Everything else on the tracker exists to protect them.

Final audio10 days out
Audio must be live at the DSPs before the playlist-pitching window closes — Spotify editorial requires the track delivered ahead of release (about a week minimum; we hold 10 days). It also needs NSI (no-sample-issues) clearance before delivery.
Final cover art10 days out
Art ships to the DSPs with the audio — no final art, no delivery. Same 10-day floor.
Music video1 wk out
The goal for the ProRes master to hit Sony Ci. Acknowledged as not always achievable — but inside a week, premiere placements start falling off.
03

The planning targets

Where each earlier date comes from, working backward from the floors.

Vinyl kickoff16 wks out
Pressing plants quote 2–4 months from lacquer to finished goods, and that's with major-label priority. Four months covers lacquers, test pressings, packaging, and freight to the warehouse.
Tracklist locked12 wks out
Everything downstream — clearances, credits, packaging art, metadata — starts from the tracklist. Lock it late and every other deadline compresses.
Sample clearances10 wks out
Clearance negotiations routinely run 6–8 weeks, and an uncleared sample stops a release cold. Flagging at tracklist-lock leaves room for the slow ones.
Mixes approved8 wks out
Leaves time for mastering, clean/explicit versions, and the NSI review before the 10-day audio floor.
Credits + splits5 wks out
Distribution needs 2–4 weeks to process and deliver a release to every platform. Credits and splits gate that submission.
Metadata + features4 wks out
Same distribution window — plus featured-artist approvals, which can't be rushed once the release is queued.
Photos + bio16 wks out
First thing every partner asks for — press, DSPs, and packaging all pull from the same shoot. Doing it once, early, feeds everything.
04

How the dates are calculated

Two rules run under the hood.

2-day cushion
Every calculated date lands 2 days ahead of the true floor. If something slips a day, it slips into the cushion — not into the release.
Weekend + holiday flags
If a deadline falls on a weekend or a US holiday, the tracker flags it — nobody's at the plant or the DSP to receive it, so the real deadline is the working day before.
